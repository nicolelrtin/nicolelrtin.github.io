#### Thoughts from Good Entertainment and On Tyranny [^1]
[^1]: By Byung Chul Han and Timothy Snyder

Most of my reviews so far include opinions on the author's writing, which is a cheap take with no real thesis. I intended to do differently and write more for Good Entertainment, but will revisit after reading more of his books. 

Aesthetic art and entertainment only differ only with their relationship to time. Entertainment is suited for a particular moment, whereas art is entertainment elevated to be “enduring”. We brand entertainment as “low brow” and art (like passion) as “high brow” entertainment, begging the question: Is aesthetic art inherently more moral?

It is no coincidence that Kafka's hunger artist (a figure of passion) and his hedonistic beast inhabit the same cage. Despite their distinct relation to being and freedom, they are both characterized by their consumption. The forms are the same, two circus figures, “trading places until the end of time”. But a life lived by the TV, unexamined, is a thoughtless escape from one infinity to the next. Tyranny feeds off the beast that is easily distracted, and willing to escape into consumerism. Enduring art enshrines messages that are essential to our nature, codifying themes that build our resilience to stress, trauma, and tyranny. One could argue that any entertainment that resonates with our human nature can be elevated into art. Possibly, it is only by facing what is finite, a life in the face of death, that we can live an authentic experience.

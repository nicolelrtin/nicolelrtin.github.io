Studying in the Berkeley libraries where he would grade my high school papers, I realize how important Mr. Berroya was to my first years of high school. I read in his obituary that "he was the teacher we all wish we had". He really was. 

I really looked forward to the short moments I was able to talk to him one-on-one. But once, he asked if he ever came off to me as dismissive. I noticed my 1:1s with him were shorter than others'. He went on to say that he really wasn't worried about me or my future. For a while this scared me—I really wasn't sure what to make of it. But in those two years, he looked out for me in more ways than I can count. I'm still amazed at how he managed to do it. He entertained my anxious planning far into the future, and correctly predicted (four years later) that I would change majors. He supported a group of seniors in starting a student government, and suggested to me, a first year, that I join their discussions. And he read to me an award he wrote for me, where he recognized all the things I had done that I had previously thought went unseen. I still have that letter.

Not just me, he did the same for all of us. He spent so much time developing relationships with each of us, on top of his proper job responsibilities. He asked each of us what our favorite song was and played it. Every Friday, he encouraged us to bring food to his class to eat, which we would enjoy while we were working or while he would give announcements. These were probably some of the best memories I had in high school. One week, a classmate brought a wagon full of tamales. Another time I stayed up making pumpkin bread. The staples, though, were bags of hot cheetos and soda. Sometimes Chuck's Donuts. At one point I had a picture of him standing in front of a presentation he made (the backdrop was the American Flag) and written on the slide was "Food + Friends = Fun". He stood next to it with a huge thumbs up.   
Of course, when we were out of line, we got to find out how loud he could be. He could make us fall in line pretty quickly. But there were so many things that he did that showed us he cared and was paying attention. In the messages he would send or in playing our favorite songs. He was a person who showed his values by example. He was the keystone species in our mentor group and in molding our school culture.

I remember us all being heartbroken when he said he was leaving our school. We had an end-of-the-year picnic (more food), and he opened up to us about the demands of being an educator and mentor. 
It was hard enough for all of us to say goodbye that first time.

<blockquote>
It was so great to see everyone (or almost everyone :) yesterday. All your smiles and hugs filled my heart, and I'm so proud of how much you've grown since freshman year.

Please remember that, above everything else, the legacy I wanted to leave you with was love. So every day when you come to school, do everything you can to love each other, love Ms. Thompson, and -- when you can -- share that love with fellow Huskies who may be struggling to be their best selves.

You are all bright, shining stars, and I know you will continue to grow and to share your light with everyone around you.

I love you.

Always,  
L
</blockquote>

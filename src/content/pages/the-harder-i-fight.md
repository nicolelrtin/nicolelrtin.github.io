More thoughts. 
Background- I was introduced to Neko Case, and in turn this book, by her conversation with Hanif Abdurraqib at SF City Arts and Lectures. While I'm not sure I would pay another $70 to attend as a twenty-something, I so appreciate the free public broadcasting and am grateful to have finally attended one(!)

For a long time, holding this book in my hands, I was thinking of giving it a low rating. I didn't really like the writing style and the material was a bit mentally tough. But then, I found I had read another 100 pages and couldn't actually put it down.

Neko Case has a very distinct writing style that, in my eyes, is wild and jumbly and reads more like a conversation. But that's exactly what makes it so compelling, I feel exactly situated in her mind. She has a distinct writing voice and inner thought that draws you into her storytelling. For a while it felt forced, but now that I've sat with it, it feels intimate. Like I am reading her plainly, as a close friend, being entrusted with her dark past and dirty secrets. 

It is hard for me not to relate this book to class. Exploring a different side of Americana through Neko's eyes gives me a greater appreciation for a life I haven't lived, and one that must resonate as true for many people. Being someone who grew up middle class, it was a story I needed to hear. I do feel like I've glimpsed into another life through her book. 

While I'm not sure exactly what I want to take from it as a whole, there are little wisdoms interspersed throughout:

a commentary on forgiveness;
<blockquote>"One of the bad ideas is that forgiveness is the ultimate act of courage... Its not a tangible "act", in the same way justice is not an act. Maybe forgiveness and justice are somehow the same state of being. Maybe forgiveness comes later, and maybe it never does."</blockquote>

and coming to pride in what you do;
<blockquote>"I couldn't untangle the difference between 'bragging' and simply wanting to exist in the world and make music and feel proud of what you do... I'd see ways you could make things and be in the world but without boasting or apologizing for you were"</blockquote>

Would recommend to those interested.

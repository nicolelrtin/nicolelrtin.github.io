## Initial thoughts
I came into this book wanting to learn more about the Osage tribe and the formation of the FBI.

I don’t read a lot of true crime, and I worry that the genre can feel exploitative and disrespectful to the victims and others affected. That was not necessarily true for this book. I really appreciated the time and detail the author included to illustrate the systematic oppression Native American tribes faced, from forced assimilation in boarding schools, to the Dawes act that stole and destroyed many tribes (that the Osage cleverly avoided). 

The amount of detail in each of the sections <i>does</i> do justice to the truly horrifying greed and corruption at the beginnings of our modern justice system, less than 100 years ago. I was pleasantly surprised by the critical exploration of Hoover’s FBI. With a death toll of over 20 Osage murders and almost no collective memory of such events, it is difficult to see how we continue to erase and neglect our own (American) history. 

However, the book is <i>so</i> detailed and dense that it almost feels like the author is just handing you a case file and an American History textbook so you can crack the case yourself. I feel myself reluctantly trudging through this book. At halfway through, I’m not sure what else there is to cover for another hundred pages. 

Gran offers an important perspective to American history, but I might DNF.

Update: Changed to 5/5 after finishing the book.

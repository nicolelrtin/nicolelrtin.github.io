Its interesting to consider how art will take on the lives of those whose hands have passed it through. I read the Goldfinch as following a rather dramatic (fictional) history of few lives who were touched by the namesake painting.

Its pretty widely encompassing, which I take to be a sign of good literature (if done well). Its a bit of an epic that way, how it follows an extended Becoming, as opposed to a coming-of-age (covering a few years). 

I love Tartt's voice, and how it can change so easily to voice different characters, different thoughts. Some people say the dialogue isn’t realistic, but I thought it was? and it was beautiful? I mean, what about it wasn’t realistic? I like the conversations that I have that sound like the ones in that book. I love how she dances around topics not pertinent to the protagonist, and it forced me to think about how we can be blind to ourselves and how we are perceived; which are often related. 

I like her exploration of chance and circumstance. While not the greatest triumph of agency, I was very invested in where things would go for Theo; he didn’t exert himself but followed what compelled him. He definitely was not the boring kind of passive that I feel like is kinda common now— he still had strong desires and followed that voice

There were a lot of stretches that I found to be dry, but I enjoyed the writing enough to happily go along for the ride.

I really enjoyed how she articulated the themes at the end. Of enduring objects that outlive our time, of how we attach meaning to objects, how beauty can fundamentally alter the grain of our reality. 

About the painting, i don’t know its literally just a painting but i get the concept. Needless to say I have a greater appreciation for Dutch paintings and still lifes. 

Overall, 4.7/5.

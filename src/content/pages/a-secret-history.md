Some thoughts, not a book report.

I read this on the heels of The Goldfinch- I was very much excited for more. Until I learned that this was Donna Tartt's first novel and recalled that The Goldfinch took Tartt ten years to write. And those facts were very evident in this book. It really felt like a premature high school version of The Goldfinch, and this one lacked a bit of depth and cohesion. It definitely did not have the scope that The Goldfinch did.
The characters were engaging. But it was hard to see them as "deep and complex", rather than purely enigmatic. That portrayal seems purposeful. I don't know if I needed to hear all the protagonist's thoughts or actions, who of all the characters had the least interesting decisions. Moreover I'm very confused what was achieved in the almost 200 pages after the central conflict to "resolution", and that awareness was clear every page.

Its entertaining in the drama, spectacle, and sometimes plainly disturbed. In that sense, it might have been more entertaining to read the sparknotes than the actual book. But in the leagues of "dark academia" and convoluted thriller/drama tv shows, The Secret History stands alone and apart. The Secret History is best read as a modern take on Greek tragedy. 

Unfortunately the most remarkable part of the book-- Julian's early lecture-- was the best part of the book. It held the most interesting prose and held all the main ideas. It was an interesting tactic, introducing all the themes so early on, before we have a chance to understand how they may play out. 

<blockquote>It's a very Greek idea, and a very profound one. Beauty is terror. Whatever we call beautiful, we quiver before it. And what could be more terrifying and beautiful, to souls like the Greeks or our own, than to lose control completely? To throw off the chains of being for an instant, to shatter the accident of our mortal selves?</blockquote>

I'm at a loss, no rating.

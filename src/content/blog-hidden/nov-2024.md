November 2024
Some election day thoughts

There have been a lot of ideas floating around in my head. The outcome of the election has made me at many times question my sanity. I've been thinking a lot about how we lost so many progressive voters. Though it has been coming for maybe 40 years now [KC], it seems to have now come to a head and none of us saw it coming. One thing that is clear is that the liberal coastal elites have really come to realize that name, now that exactly only those areas were voting for Harris and that more non-college-educated voters went to trump. 

Modern progressivism has heavily championed women's rights and racial equality; we saw many women in stem initiatives and movements to give children of color better opportunity to achieve equality. And in doing so we abandoned the language of  the American Dream, that speaks to everyone (all people) having the chance to succeed, if they work hard enough. We instead spoke heavily of needing to equalize the playing field so that the disadvantaged can achieve (equally). But in doing so, we actually began to neglect the non-college educated; and we neglected boys, and we neglected boys of color. About 20 years ago we saw equality in university entrance, but not in the workforce. Then we saw (relative) equality in the workforce, but the pay gap was still not closed. And so we pursued and pursued to close more and more gaps while the attention to men in schooling went farther and farther. And there are proposed ways to address this [Of Boys and Men]. But now we have alienated young adults who are tired of this new liberal propaganda and turned instead to their new role models and podcasters.

The morality of America used to be predominantly Christian. I think since 2008 Obama policy and the internet, we have seen a new morality emerge, which is the morality of the left. Championing racial equality, women's rights, lgtbq rights became the new fashion as 'radical' legal changes were enacted around the US. And then this language, ubiquitous, legal, and en-vogue, was then adopted by corporate america. Now companies were not only expected to uphold sustainable ecological practices, but also to hire with diversity, equity, and inclusion; to celebrate pride month, rebrand thanksgiving, and give holidays to all religious practices. The language of the Left was co-opted by corporate america and became a new set of virtuous standards [Catherine Wei]. 
Similarly, there was a push in grade school (or the left?) towards higher education. College degrees were seen as way to move up the class system, and (along the way  of the scientific revolution hundreds of years later,) we've equated academic ability with value. The aggressive increases in college enrollment also saw student debts rise and employment opportunities shrink. Still, education attainment was seen as a sign of virtue and people pursued higher education, often not as a research endeavor, but as a means to higher income or to signal worth/virtue (which in these times, are the same) [we'll get back to climate change deniers trying to earn PhDs in climate science, later]. Is the push towards higher education inherently good? Probably not, and we can go on more about that another time. But as they should, academic institutions historically have a progressive agenda that was not necessarily the learnings some attendees expected to get. 

And so with moderates and republicans funneling through higher education and corporate america, they were impressed with liberal values as moral values, and corporate america's morality became the washed out language of the left. People that didn't necessarily agree became disillusioned with the language changes and the new moralities pressed on them. And when a woman; a biracial woman; a biracial black and indian woman was proposed to them as president; one with her policies against the resurging Trumpism; I guess they left.

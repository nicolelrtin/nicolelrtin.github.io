## The "modular" resume
A brilliant suggestion I learned from a college friend is to maintain a "Modular Resume". This is not a one-page resume you send to others, this resume benefits you. The modular resume should be a living accumulation of every experience you've acquired, documented from several angles.

As you move through classes, volunteering, internships, you'll find one "resume" experience can be framed in different ways:
* Experience: CS61B
 * Technical: Programmed a deterministic agent to solve game trees by alpha-beta pruning in Java.
 * Teamwork: Led programmingimplementation and oral presentation of a four-person team project (example)

Once in a while (towards the end of the semester), take stock of the projects you have done and the causes you have spent time towards. Better yet, do it right after you complete something interesting or learn something new. Bullet point what you did, why you did it, how you did it, the effect you had, and skills you learned. Even if it is not resume-ready yet, you have a better basis for writing about it later. 

Now when you are applying for a job, cherry pick the experiences and bullet points that are most relevant to the role you're applying for. Customizing your resume for each role becomes a task of curation rather than de novo reframing.
